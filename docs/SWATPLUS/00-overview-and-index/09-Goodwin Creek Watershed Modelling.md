# 1. USDA ARS STEWARDS Dataset

The USDA Agricultural Research Service (USDA-ARS), through the Conservation Effects Assessment Project Watershed Assessment Studies, developed the [Sustaining the Earth’s Watersheds—Agricultural Research Data System (STEWARDS Version 4.0)](https://www.nrrig.mwa.ars.usda.gov/stewards/stewards.html) to facilitate public access to data collected from long-term agricultural research watersheds. STEWARDS is a web-based data visualization and delivery platform that allows users to search, explore, and download soil, water, climate, land-management, and socioeconomic data. The database currently includes observations from nearly 20 USDA-ARS research watersheds distributed across the United States, providing valuable information for watershed modeling, conservation assessment, and agricultural and environmental research.

- **`MSGC` - best for sediment-organic nitrogen method development**  
This is the strongest site if you want to improve channel N cycling with sediment interaction. The sediment variables are more detailed than MOGC, and Kjeldahl N gives you a usable proxy for organic plus reduced nitrogen pools.
- **`MOGC` - best field-to-channel watershed structure**  
MOGC is still excellent for field/plot/groundwater/stream routing, but it lacks Kjeldahl/organic N and sediment-size detail. It is better for delivery/routing than for building complex sediment-organic N pools.

# 2. MSGC modelling

## 2.1 Background

Goodwin Creek drains 2132 ha in Panola County which is in the north central part of the state of Mississippi. Drainage is westerly to Long Creek which flows into the Yocona River, one of the main rivers of the Yazoo River Basin, a tributary of the Mississippi River. 

![[09-Study area.png|600]]

The watershed is divided into ==13 subwatersheds==, which range in size from 28 to 1292 ha. The watershed is located in the loess-covered, bluff hills province just east of the Mississippi River flood plain. Elevation on the watershed ranges from 71 m (233 ft) to 128 m (420 ft) above sea level, with an ==average channel slope of 0.004 m==. The soils on the watershed consist of two major associations. One soil association is the Collins-Falaya-Grenada-Calloway association that is mapped in the terrace and flood plain locations. These soils are ==poorly to moderately well drained== and include much of the cultivated area in the watershed. The other soil association, the Loring-Grenada-Memphis association, developed on the loess ridges and hillsides. These soils are ==moderately well to well drained== on gently sloping to very steep surfaces and include most of the pasture and wooded area of the watershed. The soils are ==silty in texture== and quite ==easily eroded when the vegetation cover is removed==. The climate on the watershed is humid, with average daily maximum temperatures of about 30 C in the summer and 10 C in the winter. ==Most major runoff events occur during winter and spring seasons==. Average annual rainfall of the watershed, measured at the climatological station near the center of the watershed, is ==1440 mm==, while the ==mean annual runoff has been determined to be 145 mm== at the watershed outlet. Land use on the watershed has changed from nearly equal portions of cultivated, pasture, and wooded in 1980 to 10% cultivated at the present time. Cultivated land is primarily composed of ==cotton, soybeans, and corn==.

- Watershed characteristics:
	- Watershed area: 2132 ha
	- East-west extent: 9.08 km (5.64 miles)
	- North-south extent: 5.25 km (3.26 miles)
